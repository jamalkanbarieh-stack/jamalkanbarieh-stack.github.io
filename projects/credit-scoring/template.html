How the model supports a credit decision

Business problem

Before a bank places funds with another bank or confirms its trade-finance paper, it needs a consistent, explainable view of that counterparty's credit quality. Many regional banks carry no agency rating, so the assessment has to be built from their financial statements and applied the same way to every name.

Calculation logic

  • Nine ratios across five pillars are each graded A–E (1–5 points) against four cut-offs.
  • Weighted score = Σ points × weight. It maps to a 12-notch scale (AA to CCC), each with an indicative one-year PD.
  • A ratio that cannot be computed meaningfully (negative equity or income) scores E, so missing strength is never assumed.
  • An optional sovereign ceiling caps the result at the country rating.

Using the output

  • The grade and PD are the inputs a limit proposal and an expected-credit-loss calculation need.
  • The sensitivity chart shows which reported figures the grade hinges on, and therefore which to verify first.
  • The stress comparison shows how many notches of headroom a counterparty has before a limit review would be triggered.
  • Grades step at thresholds, so a small input change can move a ratio a full band or not at all.

Methodology

Ratios & weights: Capital 20% (CAR; equity/RWA) · Asset quality 30% (NPL ratio; NPL ÷ (equity + ECL)) · Profitability 20% (net income/RWA; pre-provision profit/RWA, annualised) · Liquidity 20% (market-funding gap/assets; liquid assets/deposits) · Efficiency 10% (cost-to-income).

Grading: each ratio scores 1 (A) to 5 (E) against four cut-offs. Ratios that are not meaningful (e.g. negative equity or negative income) score E. The weighted score maps to a 12-notch scale from AA to CCC, with an indicative PD. An optional sovereign ceiling caps the final rating.

Why asset quality carries the most weight: Syrian banks' capital ratios are inflated by FX revaluation of their structural positions. Weighting capital less stops large capital buffers from masking very weak loan books.
