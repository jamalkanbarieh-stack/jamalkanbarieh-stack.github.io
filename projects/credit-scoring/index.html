Methodology

Ratios & weights: Capital 20% (CAR; equity/RWA) · Asset quality 30% (NPL ratio; NPL ÷ (equity + ECL)) · Profitability 20% (net income/RWA; pre-provision profit/RWA, annualised) · Liquidity 20% (market-funding gap/assets; liquid assets/deposits) · Efficiency 10% (cost-to-income).

Grading: each ratio scores 1 (A) to 5 (E) against four cut-offs. Ratios that are not meaningful (e.g. negative equity or negative income) score E. The weighted score maps to a 12-notch scale from AA to CCC, with an indicative PD. An optional sovereign ceiling caps the final rating.

Why asset quality carries the most weight: Syrian banks' capital ratios are inflated by FX revaluation of their structural positions. Weighting capital less stops large capital buffers from masking very weak loan books.
