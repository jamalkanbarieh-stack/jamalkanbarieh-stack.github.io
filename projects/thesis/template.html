Investment Portfolio Management & Profitability in Syrian Islamic Banks

Does diversification pay? MBA research (Financial Management, Higher Institute of Business Administration, 2026) testing whether diversifying across instruments, currencies, sectors and geographies improves the profitability of Syria's Islamic banks, controlling for asset quality, cost efficiency and market concentration. Explore the regression results, test each hypothesis, and trace every variable bank by bank.

ⓘ
Sample: 3 Islamic banks: Al Baraka Bank Syria (2014–2025), Cham Bank and Syria International Islamic Bank (2012–2023). 12 audited annual observations each, giving 36 bank-years. Profitability is adjusted to remove unrealised FX revaluation on the structural position. Regression statistics are the published SPSS results.

Headline result

  • Cost efficiency is the one robust driver. The adjusted cost-to-income ratio is the only predictor significant in more than one model (ROAA p < .001, ROAE p = .013). It holds three ways: in the pooled coefficient, within each bank over time, and in the cross-bank ranking.
  • Diversification shows no reliable effect on returns. None of the four HHI measures is significant for ROAA or ROAE. Sector concentration is significantly negative only for the financing margin (NFIM, p = .039), and that model is itself only marginal (p = .068).
  • Why the HHIs disagree: the same index captures different economics at each bank. High currency concentration means hard currency at BBSY but Syrian pounds at SIIB, so pooled coefficients average opposing effects.

Recommendations

For Islamic banks

  • Prioritise operating cost efficiency through digital banking, product standardisation and process optimisation.
  • Avoid excessive sectoral and geographic concentration, and use newly accessible markets and instruments to diversify.
  • Where feasible, reallocate non-income-generating balances to Sharia-compliant investment agencies or short-term instruments.

For the central bank & regulators

  • Deepen the Islamic financial market with a broader range of Sharia-compliant instruments.
  • Introduce local sovereign Sukuk to widen investment opportunities and improve risk-adjusted returns.
  • Support shared banking infrastructure and sector-wide digital transformation to lower operating costs.

For future research

  • Apply panel estimators (fixed/random effects, Hausman test) to control for unobserved bank-specific effects.
  • Expand the sample as more Islamic banks publish audited statements, and extend the study period.

About this research

Research question: Does income and asset diversification improve the profitability of Islamic banks operating in Syria's high-inflation, sanctions-constrained environment?

Method: Variables were built from audited financial statements: four HHI concentration indices (portfolio composition, currency, sector, geography), the Stage 3 financing ratio, the adjusted cost-to-income ratio and an Islamic-sector competition HHI. These were regressed on three profitability measures (adjusted ROAA, adjusted ROAE and the net financing and investment margin) using pooled OLS in SPSS. The Power BI model then turned the results into a navigable evidence base, with star-schema fact tables for ratios, exposures, portfolio components and regression output.

What this view adds: a relationship explorer for any predictor/outcome pair, with pooled and within-bank correlations; calendar or study-year alignment; and the underlying exposure mix behind each HHI.
