    About this analysis

    Business question: How does each Syrian private bank compare with its peers on scale, profitability, efficiency, credit risk, liquidity and capital, and how is that changing quarter to quarter?

    Approach: Quarterly balance sheets, income statements and capital disclosures for 15 banks were compiled into a single dataset, cleaned in Power Query and modelled in Power BI. The original report has 24 pages and more than 100 DAX measures covering market share, rankings, sector benchmarks and A–E ratings. Profit figures are adjusted to strip out unrealised structural FX revaluation, which otherwise dominates Syrian bank earnings when the pound moves.

    What this view adds: any bank can be the focus, Islamic and conventional peer groups, annualised interim returns so quarters are comparable, like-for-like growth rates, a risk–return map, and a sortable league table.

    Definitions

    • ROA / ROE: adjusted net income ÷ total assets / adjusted equity, annualised for Q1–Q3
    • Cost-to-income: operating expenses (excl. ECL) ÷ adjusted operating income
    • NPL ratio: non-performing facilities ÷ gross direct facilities
    • NPL coverage: ECL provisions ÷ NPLs (n.m. when NPL ratio < 0.5%)
    • Loan-to-deposit: net direct facilities ÷ customer deposits
    • Liquidity ratio: liquid assets ÷ (liabilities + off-balance-sheet commitments)
    • Market-funds ratio: (non-customer liabilities − liquid assets) ÷ total assets; lower is safer
    • CAR: regulatory capital ÷ risk-weighted assets
    • Benchmarks: sector aggregates (Σ numerator ÷ Σ denominator); CAR and liquidity use simple averages, as in the original model
    • Ratings A–E: CAR >10.5 / 8.4 / 6.3 / 4.2%; CIR <45 / 55 / 65 / 80%; market funds <−10 / 0 / 10 / 20%
