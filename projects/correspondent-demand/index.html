Demand Estimation & Financial Model Methodology

Demand Estimation of Utilizing the Bank A – Correspondent Bank B Channel

When a bank opens a new correspondent channel, management needs to know three things before committing liquidity and operations to it: how much payment flow will actually move through it, what that does to the nostro balance, and whether the fees and liquidity carry cover the cost of running it. This case study walks through the model I built to answer those questions: a survey-based demand estimate, weighted by confidence and phased in over a 12-month adoption curve, then carried through the balance sheet and income statement to a net contribution figure. Change the scenario, filter a counterparty or currency, and move the assumption sliders to see every chart recalculate.

    5 · Methodology

    Model architecture, inputs, formulas and limitations.

    Business problem

    A new correspondent route gives the Bank's customers access to international clearing through Bank A and Correspondent Bank B. Before go-live, Treasury and management need a defensible estimate of the flows it will carry, the nostro liquidity it will tie up or release, and its contribution after costs, so they can set funding, staffing and pricing.

    Approach

    Demand is estimated bottom-up from a survey of the Bank's business units, each asked for the monthly transfers they expect once the channel is fully available. Estimates are weighted by a confidence rating, benchmarked against current activity, phased in with an adoption curve, and then run through the balance sheet and income statement.

    Decision use

    The output gives a monthly view of volumes, balances and contribution under three scenarios. It shows how much liquidity to pre-fund, when surplus balances can be placed, which demand sources pay for themselves, and which assumptions management should validate before relying on the forecast.

    Model architecture and calculation logic

    A. Historical activity assessment

    24 months of transfers through existing channels are profiled by counterparty, currency and direction: transfer count, average ticket and monthly value. Seasonal indices come from the ratio of each month to a fitted log-linear trend, averaged by calendar month and normalised to 1.00.

    B. Demand estimation

    Steady-state monthly volume = Expected no. of transfers × Average amount per transfer Expected demand = Steady-state monthly volume × Confidence rating

    The confidence rating is the estimated probability that actual steady-state volume will be at least the level stated: 90% very high, 70% high, 50% moderate, below 50% low. Current activity is the benchmark: where stated demand is more than three times what the counterparty moves today, the estimate is flagged for challenge.

    C. Utilisation forecast

    Forecast volume (t) = Expected demand × Adoption factor (t) × Seasonal index (t) × (1 + g)^(t/12) Forecast transfers (t) = Expected transfers × Confidence × Adoption factor (t) × Seasonal index (t) × (1 + g)^(t/12)

    The adoption factor is the share of steady-state demand expected to have migrated by month t. g is annual organic growth. Seasonality and growth are extensions I added for this public version.

    D. Balance projection

    Closing balance (t) = Opening (t) + ρ × max(Inward − Outward, 0) + min(Inward − Outward, 0) Settlement float (t) = Inward volume (t) × Settlement lag ÷ Days in month Average nostro balance (t) = (Opening + Closing) ÷ 2 − Settlement float Minimum operating balance (t) = max(Floor, Cover days × Outward volume ÷ Business days)

    Inward transfers raise both the nostro and amounts due to clients; outward transfers funded from deposits lower both; outward transfers funded from the Bank's own liquidity lower the nostro only. ρ (retention) is the share of net inflows that stays on the nostro instead of being drawn down or repatriated.

    Income statement and outputs

    E. Revenue estimation

    Gross fee income (t) = Σ Transfers (segment) × max(Minimum fee, Fee rate (direction, ticket tier) × Average amount) FX margin (t) = Σ Volume (currency) × Share converted × FX spread Correspondent charges (t) = Σ Transfers × (Correspondent charge + Clearing pass-through + SWIFT cost) Operating cost (t) = Cost per transfer × Σ Transfers Net fee income = Gross fee income + FX margin − Correspondent charges − Operating cost

    F. Liquidity carry and net contribution

    Average surplus = max(0, Average nostro balance − Minimum operating balance) Investment income = Average surplus × Wakala profit rate × Days ÷ 365 Net investment income = Investment income − Wakala agency fee Funding requirement = max(0, Minimum operating balance − Average nostro balance) Funding cost = Funding requirement × Funding rate × Days ÷ 365 Net liquidity carry = Net investment income − Funding cost Net contribution = Net fee income + Net liquidity carry

    Contribution is reported in absolute terms and as a return on the average surplus balance employed, so the channel can be compared with other uses of the same liquidity.

    Scenarios and sensitivity

    Three scenarios change five levers together: adoption curve, confidence weighting, organic growth, average ticket and balance retention. The tornado chart then flexes one driver at a time by ±20% to isolate its effect.

    Limitations

    • Survey-based demand is judgemental. The confidence haircut reduces optimism bias but does not remove it.
    • Adoption is a fixed curve applied to all counterparties; in practice it would differ by client type and be recalibrated after launch.
    • Balances are modelled monthly on averages. Intraday and intra-month liquidity peaks, which drive real pre-funding needs, are not captured.
    • Retention is a single parameter standing in for client behaviour that would need to be observed.
    • Fee tiers are applied to average tickets, not to each transfer; no competitive response, pricing change or compliance cost is modelled.
    • All amounts are in USD equivalent at fixed illustrative rates, so FX translation risk is outside the model.

    About this case study

    Background: as a Financial Institutions Analyst in a Treasury & Investment department I built a Correspondent Banking Channel Transaction Demand & Volume Forecasting Model. It forecast monthly transfer demand from branch-level estimates, confidence-weighted assumptions and a 12-month customer adoption curve, with projected impacts on nostro balances, fee income, liquidity carry and net contribution.

    What is shown here: the same method applied to simulated data, with the institutions anonymised. I added the historical benchmark and seasonality, the FX margin stream, the scenario set and the sensitivity analysis so the logic can be explored interactively.

    Skills demonstrated: driver-based forecasting, separating flows from stocks, linking operational volumes to the balance sheet and income statement, unit economics, scenario design and communicating assumptions to non-specialists.
